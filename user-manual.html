Chapter 1
Introduction & Overview
What the Zahran Toric Tool does and how to use it safely.

1.1 Purpose of the Application

The Zahran Toric Tool is a mobile clinical decision-support tool designed to assist ophthalmic surgeons in planning and executing toric intraocular lens (IOL) implantation. It combines pre-operative biometric calculation, intraoperative axis alignment, and post-operative outcome analysis in a single cohesive workflow.

The application follows established ophthalmic conventions including TABO axis notation (0°–180°) and the double-angle vector method (DAVM) for all astigmatism calculations.

1.2 Key Features

🧮

Toric IOL Calculator

Calculates residual astigmatism, IOL cylinder power, and optimal target axis from corneal biometry.

📷

Premium Camera Capture

Instrument-gauge dial with TABO degree markings, live tilt sensor, and centre level readout.

🎯

IOL Alignment Overlay

Real-time reference and target axis lines overlaid on the captured eye photo.

📌

Landmark Annotation

Tap-to-place vessel reference markers on the eye photo for pre-operative marking.

📊

Alpins Vector Analysis

Full post-operative outcome analysis: TIA, SIA, DV, CI, ME, AE, and IS metrics with double-angle plot.

📈

SIA Nomogram Tracker

Builds a personal SIA nomogram from your cases to personalise future calculations.

🔬

PCA Correction

Barrett-style Posterior Corneal Astigmatism correction for more accurate toric planning.

🩺

Post-Refractive Mode

Adapted planning for eyes with prior LASIK, PRK, or RK surgery.

1.3 Medical Disclaimer

All calculations must be reviewed and verified by the treating surgeon before any clinical action is taken. The developer assumes no liability for outcomes resulting from use of this application. Chapter 4
Toric Calculator
Computing optimal IOL cylinder power and alignment axis.

4.1 Entering Biometric Data

Navigate to the Toric Calculator from the Patient Detail screen. Biometric data already saved in the eye record is pre-populated automatically. The calculator requires K1/K2 powers and axes, IOL cylinder, SIA, SIA axis, and target residual astigmatism.

4.2 PCA Correction (Barrett Posterior Corneal Astigmatism)

The PCA Correction toggle applies the Barrett method, accounting for the ~0.3 D against-the-rule contribution of the posterior corneal surface not captured by anterior keratometry.

When to enable PCA Correction Enable for all patients unless you have direct posterior corneal measurements (e.g. Pentacam TCRP data).
// Double-angle vector components of anterior astigmatism
Ax = (K1 − K2) · cos(2 × K1_axis_rad)
Ay = (K1 − K2) · sin(2 × K1_axis_rad)

// Add 0.3 D ATR posterior contribution
Tx = Ax + 0.3 | Ty = Ay

// Convert back to magnitude / axis
adjustedMag = √(Tx² + Ty²)
adjustedAxis = ½ · atan2(Ty, Tx) × (180/π) [mod 180°]

4.3 Post-Refractive Surgery Mode

When the eye has had prior corneal refractive surgery, enable the Post-Refractive toggle in the eye record and select the procedure type (LASIK, PRK, or RK). A warning banner is displayed in the calculator prompting use of dedicated post-refractive formulas (e.g. Barrett True-K, Haigis-L).

Post-Refractive Cases Standard toric formulas are less reliable after corneal refractive surgery. Always cross-reference with a dedicated post-refractive IOL calculator.

4.4 Results & Surgical Plan Export

After tapping Calculate, the results panel shows: recommended target axis, expected residual astigmatism, correction index estimate, and a rotation effect chart (residual vs. IOL rotation 0°–30°).

Tap Export Plan to generate a PDF surgical plan with patient details, biometry, calculated axes, and the rotation effect chart. Share via any installed share sheet (email, AirDrop, etc.).

5.2 Leveling the Device

The app reads the device's roll angle from the gyroscope at 10 Hz. The centre circle turns green and the shutter button glows green when device tilt is ≤ ±3°.

5.3 Capture Steps

  1. 1
    Open the Camera screen from Patient Detail.
  2. 2
    Align the limbus to the main gold ring and the pupil to the cyan inner ring.
  3. 3
    Wait for the centre readout to show LEVEL (tilt ≤ ±3°).
  4. 4
    Tap the shutter button (glows green when level). The app saves the image and proceeds to the Alignment screen.

Chapter 7
Landmark Annotation Tool
Marking vessel reference points for intraoperative orientation.

7.1 Overview

Cyclorotation of the eye during surgery (typically 2°–5°) can shift the effective axis of a toric IOL. Marking pre-operative conjunctival vessel landmarks provides a reliable reference to compensate intraoperatively.

7.2 Adding Landmarks

  1. 1
    Open Landmark Annotation from Patient Detail (Annotate button) or from the Alignment Screen quick actions.
  2. 2
    The eye photo is shown full-screen. Tap any vessel to place a marker.
  3. 3
    Each marker gets a sequential number and unique colour. Colours cycle through 6 options automatically.
  4. 4
    Use Undo Last to remove the most recent marker, or Clear All to start over.
  5. 5
    Tap Save to store the landmarks in the patient record.
Clinical Tip Mark 3–4 distinct conjunctival vessels at different positions around the limbus (e.g. 3h, 6h, 9h). Vessels are more reliable than limbal marks as they are fixed relative to the underlying sclera.

7.3 Landmark List

Below the image, a list shows all landmarks with their sequential number, colour, and normalised coordinates (x%, y%) as a percentage of image dimensions. Coordinates are stored resolution-independently.

Chapter 8
Alpins Vector Analysis
Quantifying post-operative astigmatism outcomes.

8.1 What is Alpins Analysis?

The Alpins method is the internationally recognised standard for reporting astigmatism surgery outcomes. It uses double-angle vector mathematics to quantify the relationship between what was planned (TIA), what was achieved (SIA), and what remains (DV). Access from the Alpins button on Patient Detail — TIA is pre-filled from the eye's corneal data.

Pre-op vs Post-op use For real post-operative analysis, enter the patient's post-op refraction cylinder as the SIA values. The pre-filled SIA (from IOL cylinder) gives a theoretical analysis only.

8.2 Metrics Reference

TIA — Target-Induced Astigmatism
The astigmatic change the surgery was planned to induce. Derived from pre-op corneal data and IOL plan.
SIA — Surgically-Induced Astigmatism
The astigmatic change actually induced. Measured from post-op refraction cylinder.
DV — Difference Vector (residual)
The vector from TIA endpoint to SIA endpoint. Ideal: 0.00 D.

8.4 Double-Angle Plot

The polar plot shows TIA, SIA, and DV vectors in double-angle space (axes doubled to convert 0°–180° TABO to a full circle).

  • Blue dashed — TIA vector
  • Green dashed — SIA vector
  • Red solid — DV (TIA tip to SIA tip)
  • Concentric grid at 33%, 67%, and 100% of maximum magnitude
Chapter 9
SIA Nomogram Tracker
Building your personalised surgically-induced astigmatism profile.

9.1 Overview

Every surgeon induces a slightly different amount of astigmatism through their incision. The SIA Nomogram tracks your personal achieved SIA across cases and calculates your vector mean, allowing personalised future calculations. Access from the Patient List → SIA button.

9.2 Adding a Case

  1. 1
    Tap + Add Case.
  2. 2
    Enter Planned SIA — magnitude and axis you expected to induce.
  3. 3
    Enter Achieved SIA — from post-op refraction.
  4. 4
    Add optional notes (incision type, wound size, etc.).
  5. 5
    Tap Save. The mean is recalculated instantly.

9.3 Mean SIA Display

The top of the screen shows your personal Vector Mean SIA (mean magnitude, mean axis, case count). Each case is colour-coded by accuracy: Green ≤0.25 D · Yellow ≤0.50 D · Red >0.50 D difference.

Recommendation Build your nomogram with at least 20 cases before using the mean SIA in routine calculations. Consider separate profiles for different incision locations.

Chapter 11
Clinical Reference
Quick-reference formulae and axis conventions.

11.1 TABO Axis Convention

DegreesClock PositionDirection
0° / 180°3h / 9hHorizontal
45°1:30hOblique up-right
90°12hVertical (superior)
135°10:30hOblique up-left

WTR (with-the-rule): steep meridian near 90°.   ATR (against-the-rule): steep meridian near 0°/180°.

11.2 Double-Angle Vector Method (DAVM)

J0 = −(C/2) · cos(2 × axis_rad) // WTR/ATR component
J45 = −(C/2) · sin(2 × axis_rad) // Oblique component

// Vector sum of astigmatisms A and B:
Rx = A.C·cos(2·A.axis) + B.C·cos(2·B.axis)
Ry = A.C·sin(2·A.axis) + B.C·sin(2·B.axis)
Result.mag = √(Rx² + Ry²)
Result.axis = ½ · atan2(Ry, Rx) · (180/π) [mod 180°]

12.2 Data & Privacy

All patient data is stored locally on your device only. No data is transmitted to any server or third party. The app does not require an internet connection. Uninstalling the app will permanently delete all stored patient data — export records as PDFs before uninstalling.

Data Backup The Zahran Toric Tool does not currently include a cloud backup feature. Export surgical plans as PDFs for important cases to maintain a record outside the app.
